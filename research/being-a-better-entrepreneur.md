## Corpus and method

- **Verified source count:** exactly **34** items in `/tmp/henikoff-corpus-v2.json` have `track == "Being A Better Entrepreneur"` (34 of 80 total corpus items).
- **Coverage:** every matching item is reviewed below, in corpus order. The review uses the supplied transcript/prose rather than reconstructing advice from titles alone.
- **Method:** each item is reduced to a principle, source-specific evidence, an operational framework, cautions, and candidate downstream skill tags. Transcript language is paraphrased; this document does not reproduce transcripts.
- **Source note:** corpus v2 includes the complete visible timed-transcript segments for eight videos that were cut off in the first collection. The supplied transcript and prose remain the review basis; item 5 is still a promotional announcement without the referenced book's substantive framework.
- **Editorial stance:** examples are treated as teaching cases, not proof of universal causal claims. Financial, accounting, tax, securities, compensation, and employment advice should be converted into decision prompts and escalation rules, not authoritative professional advice.

## Item-by-item review

### 1. [Don’t trust Google for your customer retention metrics](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/dont-trust-google-for-your-customer-retention-metrics)

*Module: From the trenches — tricks to operate better; published 2022-03-24*

- **Distilled principle:** measure retention against customers who were actually eligible to return, not as the share of the current period's traffic or purchases that happens to come from returning users.
- **Evidence in the source:** the example has 100 prior-period customers, 90 returning customers, and 500 total current-period customers. The source contrasts 90/100 eligible customers with 90/500 current customers, showing how acquisition volume can mechanically distort the latter ratio.
- **Actionable framework:** define the return event; define an eligibility cohort and lookback window that match the product's purchase cadence; calculate returned eligible customers / eligible customers; keep acquisition metrics separate; compare cohort curves over time; annotate changes in channel mix or product behavior.
- **Cautions / edge cases:** a browser “returning user” is not necessarily a retained paying customer. Cookies, identity stitching, seasonal purchasing, reactivation, multiple accounts, cohort censoring, and differing expected purchase intervals matter. Google Analytics behavior and labels are product-version dependent; the skill should teach denominator design rather than hard-code a critique of one vendor. Eligible-cohort return is a behavioral proxy for satisfaction, not direct proof of it.
- **Proposed skill tags:** `retention-cohorts`, `metric-denominators`, `customer-health`, `unit-economics`

### 2. [How Defining Purpose Yields Results](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-how-defining-purpose-yields-results)

*Module: From the trenches — tricks to operate better; published 2021-12-21*

- **Distilled principle:** agree on a measurable definition of success before committing time or resources.
- **Evidence in the source:** the same move is applied to three settings: a meeting, an enterprise pilot, and use of a seed round. The pilot example asks the buyer to specify the criteria that would justify a larger contract before the pilot begins.
- **Actionable framework:** before the activity, write the decision or outcome sought; identify the decision-maker; agree on two to five observable criteria, baseline, target, deadline, and evidence source; document dependencies and the next decision; revisit the criteria if conditions change; close by comparing results with the agreed definition.
- **Cautions / edge cases:** the claim that this works nearly every time is anecdotal. A successful pilot does not eliminate procurement, budget, security, politics, or strategic-change risk. Investors usually cannot credibly precommit to lead a future round, and optimizing solely for fundraising milestones can distort company priorities. Include learning goals when outcomes cannot yet be known.
- **Proposed skill tags:** `definition-of-success`, `pilot-design`, `meeting-design`, `milestone-planning`, `fundraise-readiness`

### 3. [How to Learn from Churn](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-how-to-learn-from-churn)

*Module: From the trenches — tricks to operate better; published 2021-05-20*

- **Distilled principle:** treat churn as both a compounding economic variable and a structured source of customer learning.
- **Evidence in the source:** a two-company model holds monthly acquisition and price constant while changing churn, then a company example segments churn reasons, tests an intervention, and reads signup cohorts over time. The source reports much stronger later-cohort retention after the change.
- **Actionable framework:** specify logo, revenue, or usage churn and its period; build cohort retention curves; collect and code cancellation reasons; rank causes by prevalence, value at risk, and fixability; form an intervention hypothesis; run a controlled or staged test; monitor cohort retention, expansion, support load, and acquisition quality; update LTV only after sufficient maturation.
- **Cautions / edge cases:** “halve churn, double revenue” holds only under simplifying assumptions such as stable acquisition, price, margin, and constant hazard, and usually describes a long-run steady state rather than an immediate result. Observational before/after cohorts can be confounded by seasonality, channel mix, or customer maturity. Tiny cohorts and projections of incomplete cohorts can mislead.
- **Proposed skill tags:** `churn-diagnostics`, `retention-cohorts`, `experiment-design`, `ltv-modeling`

### 4. [The Right KPIs Matter](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-the-right-kpis-matter)

*Module: From the trenches — tricks to operate better; published 2021-05-05*

- **Distilled principle:** choose actionable metrics along the causal funnel and design denominators so one stage cannot look better merely because another stage changed.
- **Evidence in the source:** an ecommerce company replaces social follower count and weekly sales dollars with unique site visitors, new customers, and the fraction of eligible recent first-time buyers who repeat. The retention denominator is deliberately insulated from current-week acquisition volume.
- **Actionable framework:** draw the business funnel; select one controllable leading metric per critical stage; define entity, event, cohort, window, denominator, owner, and update cadence; add outcome and guardrail metrics; test whether a metric can be gamed; review trends and decisions, not just snapshots.
- **Cautions / edge cases:** the proposed six-week repeat window is business-specific. A narrow KPI set can ignore margin, refunds, customer quality, brand, capacity, or existing-customer revenue. “Unique” identities are imperfect. KPI movement is not automatically causal, so pair the scorecard with experiments or operational evidence.
- **Proposed skill tags:** `kpi-architecture`, `funnel-metrics`, `metric-denominators`, `anti-vanity-metrics`

### 6. [How to Get Better Introductions](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/how-to-get-better-introductions)

*Module: From the trenches — tricks to operate better; published 2021-01-26*

- **Distilled principle:** make an introduction easy and valuable by supplying a concise, transparent note in the requester's voice and letting the connector obtain the recipient's consent.
- **Evidence in the source:** the connector forwards the requester's explanation of fit, privately asks whether the recipient wants the introduction, and only then connects both parties. This changes the request from an unexplained favor into a relevance-screened opportunity.
- **Actionable framework:** research the recipient; write a forwardable note addressed to the connector; state who you are, why this specific recipient is relevant, mutual value, and the narrow requested next step; avoid impersonating the connector or writing praise on their behalf; connector asks permission; on acceptance, make the connection and exit; requester follows up promptly and reports the outcome.

### 7. [Reading Between the Lines](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-reading-between-the-lines)

*Module: From the trenches — tricks to operate better; published 2020-03-12*

- **Distilled principle:** in high-stakes communication, audit the plausible inferences created by timing, omissions, and context—not only the literal words.
- **Evidence in the source:** a delayed acquisition call is interpreted as negotiating gamesmanship, while a small proposed option pool is interpreted as weak hiring ambition. In both cases, the receiver assigns meaning the sender did not intend.
- **Actionable framework:** identify audience incentives and anxieties; list two or three likely interpretations; state the real constraint or intent without oversharing; pair a delay or boundary with a concrete next step; test sensitive messages with a disinterested reviewer; ask the receiver to confirm understanding when stakes are high.
- **Cautions / edge cases:** inference is not fact, so clarify before reacting. The cited “standard” option-pool percentage is stage-, market-, and hiring-plan dependent and should not become a universal benchmark. Communication review should reduce ambiguity, not manipulate the other party or reveal privileged strategy.
- **Proposed skill tags:** `ambiguity-audit`, `negotiation-communication`, `stakeholder-empathy`, `message-review`

### 8. [Paying for Services with Equity](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-paying-for-services-with-equity)

*Module: From the trenches — tricks to operate better; published 2020-02-11*

- **Distilled principle:** separate the market value of contractor services from the later purchase of equity so scope and consideration remain explicit.
- **Evidence in the source:** the contractor invoices work in dollars; at a financing, the company pays the accumulated obligation and the contractor invests the same amount at the round price. The proposed structure aims to avoid an open-ended promise such as a fixed company percentage for an uncertain project.
- **Actionable framework:** define scope, rate, acceptance, invoicing, cap, stop-work rights, IP assignment, and what happens if financing never occurs; record the payable; have qualified counsel and tax/accounting advisers design any equity issuance; document board approvals, securities exemptions, valuation, tax reporting, and capitalization; never imply the round investor's terms automatically apply.

A circular exchange of checks is not automatically tax-clean, cash-neutral, permitted, or sufficient; the provider may owe ordinary income tax and the company may have withholding/reporting obligations. Financing may not happen, the provider may not qualify to invest, and deferred wages may violate law. The skill must require professional review and present cash, milestone equity, options, warrants, or a properly documented payable as alternatives—not execute the source's example as a recipe.
- **Proposed skill tags:** `services-for-equity`, `contractor-scope`, `advisor-gated`, `cap-table-governance`

### 9. [3 Things You Need to Have a Successful Business](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-3-things-you-need-to-have-a-successful-business)

### 13. [Do You KNOW the Difference between Gross Profit and Contribution Margin?](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-do-you-know-the-difference-between-gross-profit-and-contribution-margin)

*Module: Financial Modeling; published 2019-11-21*

- **Distilled principle:** distinguish company-level gross profit from decision-oriented contribution economics at an offering, customer, or unit level, and define exactly which costs are included.
- **Evidence in the source:** a warehouse manager is used to illustrate a cost that may appear in company gross profit but not a single-product calculation, while shipping and packaging are associated more directly with a product.

### 19. [Financial Modeling Part 3 – Modeling Your Expenses](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-financial-modeling-part-3-modeling-your-expenses)

*Module: Financial Modeling; published 2019-08-15*

### 22. [Financial Modeling Part 1 – What is a Financial Model?](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-financial-modeling-part-1-what-is-a-financial-model)

*Module: Financial Modeling; published 2019-07-02*

- **Distilled principle:** projections are outputs; a financial model is the explicit system of assumptions and relationships that produces them and permits safe “what if” testing.
- **Evidence in the source:** the source contrasts accounting statements as a rear-view mirror, KPIs as a current-state dashboard, and a driver-based model as a forward view. ### 24. [Founder Vesting, RSU’s and 83(b) Elections – Making Sense of the Mess](https://startups.henikoff.com/lesson/math-101-founder-vesting-rsus-and-83b-elections-making-sense-of-the-mess)

## Cross-source themes

### 1. The core operating loop is assumptions → actions → outcomes → learning

The most durable idea across the corpus is not “make a spreadsheet”; it is to expose a causal theory of the business. The budgeting and financial-modeling pieces connect assumptions to operational detail, financial outputs, actual results, and revised beliefs (items 10, 17, 19, 21–22, 31–34). The same loop appears outside finance: define pilot success in advance (2), classify churn and test a remedy (3), ask for disconfirming product evidence (23), and stage capital behind payback evidence (29).

**Skill implication:** every analytic skill should request a decision, express assumptions with units and confidence, show driver relationships, prescribe validation, preserve the original prediction, and end with what was learned. A static template without this loop would contradict the sources' strongest lesson.

### 2. Marginal and cohort views are usually more diagnostic than blends

The retention denominator focuses on eligible prior customers rather than current-period mix (1, 4). Churn is read by signup cohort (3). New-customer revenue exposes deterioration hidden in the installed-base average (28). CAC is segmented by channel, promotion, and volume (26–27). This is a coherent doctrine: totals describe the stock of the business, while recent cohorts and marginal response often reveal the direction of travel.

**Skill implication:** diagnostic outputs should show the aggregate for context, then decompose by cohort/channel/segment and distinguish average from marginal economics. They should also warn that smaller slices are noisier and need uncertainty estimates.

### 3. Unit economics, repeatability, and liquidity must be evaluated together

Items 9, 18–19, 26–27, 29, and 31 reject growth as a standalone success metric. A motion is attractive only if customer economics are sound, it remains repeatable at the next volume, payback fits within runway, and the company survives the interim cash trough. Fundraising can create value despite dilution if it funds a validated response curve, but only under scenario uncertainty.

**Skill implication:** any growth or capital recommendation should include marginal CAC, contribution LTV, payback, retention, capacity, cash low point, runway, uncertainty, and a staged test. It should not rely on a single LTV/CAC threshold.

### 4. Intellectual honesty must be designed into metrics and conversations

The corpus repeatedly prefers evidence that can make the founder uncomfortable: churn reasons (3), non-gameable KPIs (4), recent-cohort economics (28), candid product feedback (23), explicit misses in investor communication (15), and precise revenue definitions (20). This is less a personality trait than a system design problem: choose sensitive metrics, make criticism safe, and preserve prior commitments.

**Skill implication:** add an “evidence against the current story” section, a metric-gaming test, data-quality notes, and an explicit unknowns list to major skills.

### 5. Communication is an operating control

Defining success (2), double-opt-in introductions (6), auditing unintended implications (7), and predictable investor updates (15) all reduce coordination error. The common mechanism is audience-aware clarity: name the objective, supply relevant context, make the requested decision explicit, and confirm understanding.

**Skill implication:** a founder-communications skill can use one message architecture across meetings, pilots, introductions, negotiations, and updates, with mode-specific templates and confidentiality checks.

### 6. Focus is a constraint-management problem, not mere refusal

The product–customer–cash loop (14), the three-part quality-of-growth test (9), and runway-based capital allocation (29) all ask what constraint an initiative relieves and when. The sources favor disciplined focus, while the better interpretation preserves bounded experiments and non-revenue necessities.

**Skill implication:** initiative triage should classify must-do risk work, bottleneck relief, fast-payback actions, and capped option-creating experiments rather than simply label everything outside immediate revenue a distraction.

### 7. Ownership matters more than artifact format

The financial-modeling series argues that founders should understand the business mechanism and every material assumption (22, 31–34). Its anti-template rhetoric is best understood as opposition to black boxes, not to reusable scaffolding.

**Skill implication:** the skill package should provide prompts, schemas, checks, and blank structures; it should require users to supply company-specific drivers and explain them back. Avoid a “magic model” populated with silent defaults.

## Tensions and apparent contradictions

1. **Build from scratch vs. ship reusable skills/templates.** Items 33–34 reject copying a generic model, while the project goal is reusable expertise. Resolve this by shipping a *model-building workflow* and schema, not a pre-populated universal forecast. Examples are tests, never defaults.
2. **Maniacal focus vs. experimentation.** Item 14 filters distractions aggressively; items 3, 21, 23, 26, and 30 advocate tests. Resolve with a fixed experiment budget and explicit kill/scale criteria tied to the current constraint.
3. **Under-promise/over-deliver vs. intellectual honesty.** Item 15 can encourage conservative commitments; items 23 and 28 demand candor. The synthesis should use calibrated ranges, assumptions, and prompt disclosure—not sandbagging.
4. **Spend more on validated acquisition vs. protect runway.** Items 18–19 show that higher spend can raise terminal value but create a fatal interim trough; item 29 prioritizes cash before cash-out. Recommendations must optimize the whole cash path and financing risk, not terminal cash alone.
5. **Price as a value signal vs. value as product truth.** Item 30 shows price shaping perception, while items 12 and 23 insist on real pain and disconfirming evidence. Price tests should follow value validation and include retention/quality guardrails.
6. **Average metrics for reporting vs. marginal metrics for decisions.** Items 20 and 31 support standardized statements and summaries, while items 1, 26–28 favor cohorts and marginal response. Use both: reconciled aggregates for accountability, decompositions for diagnosis.
7. **Repeatability vs. pulse testing.** Item 9 prizes repeatable motions; item 26 proposes a short burst to infer scale. Treat the pulse as a stress test that still requires sustained replication and downstream quality measurement.
8. **Founder value vs. company/stakeholder outcomes.** Item 18 explicitly models founder equity value, while financing and compensation affect employees and investors too. Scenario outputs should include company survival, stakeholder dilution, preference stacks, and governance—not founder proceeds alone.
9. **“Cash is fact” vs. complete financial truth.** Item 34 correctly emphasizes liquidity, but cash balances can be restricted, unreconciled, or offset by obligations. Preserve the intuition while requiring balance-sheet and cash-flow reconciliation.

## Claims requiring current verification or professional disclaimers

### Legal, tax, securities, and employment

- **Items 8, 16, 24, and 25 must be advisor-gated.** Do not turn their examples into self-executing legal steps.
- The contractor invoice/investment exchange in item 8 requires current advice on compensation income, securities exemptions, valuation, corporate approvals, worker classification, wage law, withholding/reporting, and what happens without a financing.
- Item 24's terminology should be corrected: restricted founder stock is not the same as an RSU, and 83(b) eligibility is instrument-specific. Current deadlines, filing methods, proof requirements, tax rates, and option treatment must be verified.
- Founder and employee equity guidance should surface vesting, repurchase, acceleration, IP, approvals, fully diluted ownership, instrument type, tax uncertainty, and jurisdiction.
- Compensation flexibility and vague startup roles must not be used to evade wage, overtime, discrimination, disclosure, or worker-protection requirements.

### Accounting and financial definitions

- Item 13's gross-profit/contribution-margin distinction is a managerial simplification; cost classification and non-GAAP measures need explicit policies and reconciliation.
- Item 20's MRR, ARR, bookings, contracted revenue, and recognized revenue definitions must be company-specific and accounting-consistent.
- Item 31's simple LTV/churn formula and “ratio above 1” criterion are not universal viability rules.
- Item 32's EBITDA definition is incomplete because it omits depreciation.
- Scenario valuations and multiples in item 18 are illustrative, not present-day benchmarks; preference terms can dominate simple dilution math.
- All models should state that forecasts are conditional, not promises, and should retain downside/liquidity scenarios.

### Dated market or tool claims

- Analytics vendor labels and identity mechanics in item 1 have changed over product generations; retain the denominator lesson, not vendor-specific UI claims.
- Channel examples and labels such as Google AdWords, Facebook acquisition, and specific platform costs are historical examples.
- The option-pool percentage described as standard in item 7 is not a universal current benchmark.
- The assertion in item 34 that Excel signals competence and Google Sheets does not is dated and should be removed. Prefer tool-agnostic control requirements.
- The item 5 book price, named-company status/exit narratives, and claims about current company outcomes should not enter evergreen skills.

## Recommended package-level design rules

1. **Use a common intake:** company stage, business model, customer/unit, decision, time horizon, available data, cash constraint, and jurisdiction where relevant.
2. **Separate facts, assumptions, calculations, and judgments:** every output should visibly label these categories.
3. **Show definitions before numbers:** especially for customers, retention, churn, revenue, CAC, LTV, margin, bookings, and runway.
4. **Pair aggregate and diagnostic views:** total/company metrics for reconciliation; cohort/channel/marginal views for decisions.
5. **Preserve uncertainty:** ranges, confidence, scenario triggers, and missing-data flags should replace false precision.
6. **Always include a validation loop:** prediction, test, actual, variance, learning, and next action.
7. **Use current-tool neutrality:** no dependency on a particular analytics vendor, ad platform, spreadsheet, BI product, or investor convention.
8. **Gate regulated advice:** tax/legal/accounting/employment topics produce issue lists and professional handoffs, not definitive personalized advice.
9. **Avoid anecdote as benchmark:** source examples should illustrate mechanisms, never seed universal conversion rates, multiples, option pools, tax rates, or payback targets.
10. **Retain provenance:** each skill should identify the Henikoff items it synthesizes and distinguish source teaching from added safety/editorial guardrails.

## Bottom-line editorial assessment

This track has unusually strong coherence around driver-based management, cohort/marginal analysis, liquidity, and intellectually honest communication. Its best material can support a high-quality universal founder operating toolkit. The financial-modeling sequence is the deepest source cluster and should be the package's spine. The communication, retention, CAC, pricing, and capital-allocation items layer naturally onto it.

The weaker or riskier material is also clear: the book announcement has insufficient substance; some accounting definitions are simplified; the 2019 equity/tax explanation contains a consequential restricted-stock/RSU conflation; and several platform, valuation, tax-rate, option-pool, and spreadsheet-convention claims are dated or context-specific. A faithful skill package should preserve the reasoning patterns while correcting terminology, exposing uncertainty, and installing explicit professional-advice boundaries.
