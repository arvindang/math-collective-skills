# MATH Build Channel Partnerships

Build a channel as a measurable mutual-value system, not as an audience-size
promise. Start with the end customer, then test value for the partner and the
startup.

## Load the basis

Read [the source map](references/source-map.md) before giving source-backed
advice. Do not reproduce transcripts or turn source anecdotes into benchmarks.
Use [the partnership brief](assets/channel-partnership-brief.md) as the default
output shape.

## Collect inputs

Request only missing inputs that can change the recommendation:

- Company stage, business model, jurisdiction, regulated sector, and decision
  deadline.
- Narrow ideal customer profile, observed problem, buying process, and current
  acquisition evidence.
- Candidate partners, existing relationships, and any active discussions.
- Desired partner motion: referral, reseller, sales, marketing, integration,
  marketplace, association, community, or another named mechanism.
- End-customer benefit, partner benefit, startup outcome, delivery burden, and
  economic constraints.
- Available funnel, conversion, retention, sales-cycle, margin, support, and
  concentration data.
- Known security, data-sharing, brand, exclusivity, procurement, or compliance
  constraints.

Label unavailable inputs as assumptions or evidence gaps. Ask one compact round
of questions when critical facts are missing; otherwise proceed with explicit
ranges and unknowns.

## Run the workflow

### 1. Gate channel readiness

Confirm that the team can name the customer, the problem, the current buying
behavior, and why a partner improves the customer outcome. Distinguish evidence
from hoped-for reach. If the ICP or demand is untested, recommend a bounded
discovery or pilot before a scaled channel program.

Classify who discovers, influences, sells, signs, bills, implements, supports,
and renews the customer. Do not use “partnership” to hide an undefined motion.

### 2. Define the ideal partner profile

Map products, services, associations, communities, events, and platforms that
already serve the ICP or an adjacent job. Score each candidate on:

- Customer overlap and relevance.
- Incremental end-customer value.
- Partner value: revenue, retention, engagement, contract value, or another
  explicit outcome.
- Startup value: qualified demand, conversion, sales velocity, retention, or
  another explicit outcome.
- Champion access, decision authority, internal incentives, and formal program
  fit.
- Enablement, integration, implementation, support, and reporting burden.
- Margin, payback, attribution, sales-cycle, concentration, brand, security,
  privacy, regulatory, and exclusivity risk.
- Evidence strength and cost of validation.

Use relative ratings with written evidence; do not manufacture precise scores.
State disqualifiers and rank a short validation queue.

### 3. Form and test the joint value proposition

Write one plain sentence covering the combined customer benefit, partner
benefit, startup benefit, and delivery mechanism. Test it with friendly,
representative candidates. Record their language, objections, required proof,
and decision process. Revise the thesis rather than treating polite interest as
demand.

### 4. Plan engagement

Identify the likely champion, economic buyer, approvers, implementers, and
internal detractors. Explain how the champion advances a legitimate internal
goal. Prefer permissioned introductions; provide a concise forwardable note and
make declining easy. Define pipeline stages, owners, next actions, and evidence
required to advance.

### 5. Prepare the business-term issue list

Clarify sales versus marketing responsibility; lead ownership and attribution;
closing, contracting, billing, renewal, and support; enablement and training;
integration and data access; brand use; payment and incentives; reporting;
success measures; review cadence; termination; and transition obligations.

Produce an issue list or business brief, not legal language. Route exclusivity,
MFN, referrals, revenue share, IP, licensing, indemnity, liability, privacy,
data processing, antitrust, sector rules, and employment questions to qualified
counsel or specialists.

### 6. Design a staged launch

Define the shared purpose, baseline, target, owner, evidence source, deadline,
and next decision. Start with the smallest credible pilot when uncertainty is
high. Translate the agreement into enablement, deliverables, instrumentation,
early wins, reporting, and a review with the champion and economic buyer.

Track customer outcomes as well as leads and revenue. Set continue, change,
pause, and stop rules. Include a customer transition plan before creating
dependency on one partner.

## Stop or ask

Stop and ask for direction when:

- No specific end customer or customer benefit can be named.
- The arrangement is one-sided, misaligned with the partner's incentives, or
  depends on misleading customers.
- No accountable owner, measurable outcome, attribution method, or exit path
  exists.
- Sensitive personal, customer, employee, or partner data would be exposed
  without authority and an approved handling plan.
- A regulated, exclusive, high-concentration, or material contract decision
  lacks appropriate business and professional review.
- The user asks for signatures, binding legal terms, or external outreach that
  has not been expressly authorized.

## Output contract

Deliver:

- Decision and confidence.
- Facts, assumptions, unknowns, and freshness date.
- ICP and channel-readiness finding.
- Ideal partner profile and ranked candidate matrix.
- Joint value proposition and validation plan.
- Stakeholder map and engagement sequence.
- Business-term issue list and professional-review handoffs.
- Pilot/launch scorecard with owners and continue/change/stop rules.
- Concentration, data, brand, operational, and customer-transition risks.
- Next three actions with owners and dates.
- Handoff naming the next skill, the partnership artifact it should consume,
  and the commercial, attribution, or legal assumptions still unresolved.

End every output with **Watch/read next** and one to three task-relevant direct
source links selected from the source map. Put nothing after that section.

## Boundaries

- Verify current partner programs, product capabilities, leadership, terms,
  policies, and contact paths from authoritative sources; record the access
  date. Mark unverified claims when current research is unavailable.
- Minimize and redact personal or confidential data. Do not expose contact
  details, customer lists, contract terms, or employee information without
  authorization.
- Treat audience reach, conversion, partner activation, and order-of-magnitude
  growth claims as hypotheses. Require attribution and downstream quality.
- Do not give legal, privacy, employment, accounting, tax, antitrust, or
  regulatory conclusions. Produce issues and questions for qualified advisers.
- Preserve the stakeholder-trust lesson: inspect promises, delivery
  touchpoints, incentives, workforce support, leadership behavior, and data
  custody. Do not claim that trust guarantees retention or financial returns.
