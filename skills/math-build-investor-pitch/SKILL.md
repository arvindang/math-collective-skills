# Build an Investor Pitch

Turn operating evidence into a focused investment case that makes the future legible without overstating certainty.

## Load the source guidance

Read [references/source-map.md](references/source-map.md) before drafting. Use [assets/investor-pitch-brief-template.md](assets/investor-pitch-brief-template.md) as the default working artifact. Do not reproduce source prose or quotations.

## Gather inputs

Collect:

- Target investor or investor profile, including stage, thesis, check size, geography, and known decision criteria
- Company stage and round objective
- Customer, buyer, user, painful problem, solution, and why now
- Product evidence, traction, customer learning, retention, and revenue history
- Acquisition model, channel-level CAC where available, LTV method, payback, margin, and sales cycle
- Bottom-up and current external market evidence with sources and dates
- Business model, competition, differentiation, and remaining risks
- Founder/team advantage supported by relevant evidence
- Raise amount, runway, dated milestones, and use-of-funds model
- Existing deck, teaser, model, data-room materials, or prior investor feedback

Mark each claim as observed fact, customer statement, calculation, forecast, hypothesis, or unknown. Never invent traction, customers, pipeline, partnerships, market evidence, credentials, or urgency.

## Run the workflow

### 1. Define the meeting objective

Identify the specific audience and next action. Treat a first meeting as an opportunity to earn deeper diligence, not to transmit every fact. Adapt to the investor's actual screening workflow; do not automatically withhold a deck when asynchronous review is required.

### 2. Build the evidence ledger

For every proposed headline, attach supporting evidence, its as-of date, and the strongest honest caveat. Flag unsupported claims for removal, research, or explicit labeling as hypotheses.

Normalize metric definitions before using them. Disaggregate blended CAC by channel and show trends or cohorts when possible. Do not present immature modeled LTV as observed fact.

### 3. Draft the investment thesis

Express the case in three or four memorable claims:

1. A specific customer has a consequential problem now.
2. The company has a differentiated, credible way to solve it.
3. Current evidence supports a large enough opportunity and an acquisition path.
4. This round funds a measurable step toward that future.

Keep only claims needed to earn the next step. Move supporting depth to an appendix.

### 4. Apply the three passes

- Essence: rank facts, keep the few headlines that must be remembered, and remove repetition.
- Empathy: answer the target investor's likely decision questions and acknowledge what remains unproven.
- Emotion: name the warranted response for each section, such as trust in the team or confidence in the next milestone. Ensure the evidence earns it; never use emotion to conceal risk.

### 5. Structure the narrative

Use a flexible sequence rather than a mandatory slide count:

1. One-line company and customer
2. Painful problem and why now
3. Product or approach
4. Current evidence and customer learning
5. Market: bottom-up beachhead plus current external cross-check
6. Go-to-market and unit economics
7. Business model and competition
8. Team advantage
9. Forward de-risking path
10. Raise, runway, dated outcomes, and immediate ask

Spend proportionally more time on the future path than on repeating the problem and product. Treat the source's problem/solution/future time split as a rehearsal heuristic, not a deck law.

### 6. Prepare the first meeting

Create concise, evidence-linked answers for customer, problem, solution, timing, team fit, differentiation, acquisition, traction, economics, market, competition, risks, and milestones. Allow the investor's questions to change the slide order. Prepare appendix evidence so direct answers do not become evasive narratives.

### 7. Produce collateral variants

When requested, derive:

- A one-page teaser that qualifies interest without becoming a compressed wall of text
- A presentation deck outline with one job and one main claim per slide
- A leave-behind that preserves definitions, source dates, and caveats
- A rehearsal script or objection log

Use the same factual ledger across every version. Do not create inconsistent numbers or claims merely to improve conversion.

## Stop and ask

Stop and ask focused questions when the target audience, round objective, customer/problem, traction basis, or ask is missing. Ask before treating a pipeline, partnership, market estimate, or customer quote as verified evidence.

Stop and flag conflicts when deck figures disagree with the model, cap table, or source documents. Do not resolve the discrepancy by choosing the more attractive number.

Ask before including confidential customer information, personal data, regulated claims, security details, unpublished financing terms, or material information not intended for the audience. Do not send, publish, or upload the pitch without explicit authorization.

## Apply professional and freshness boundaries

Treat projections, valuation, unit economics, and market size as assumptions that require definitions and dates. Refresh market reports, investor thesis, portfolio, check size, and comparable data before live use. If current evidence is unavailable, mark it as a research gap.

Do not give legal, tax, accounting, securities, or investment advice. Do not imply that a deck, disclaimer, confidentiality legend, or data-room access policy creates legal protection. Route offering claims, solicitation language, customer disclosure, financial presentation, and regulated-product statements to qualified professionals.

## Deliver the output

Provide:

1. Audience and meeting objective
2. Three or four remembered claims
3. Evidence ledger with gaps and dates
4. One-paragraph investment narrative
5. Slide-by-slide story map
6. Dated raise-to-milestone statement
7. Likely investor questions and direct answers
8. Appendix/data requests and rehearsal priorities
9. Optional teaser or collateral variant requested by the user
10. Handoff to the investor-process skill with the pitch artifact, evidence
    gaps, audience assumptions, and claims that need verification

Separate facts from forecasts and make all calculated claims inspectable. Lead with the revised artifact, then briefly explain material edits or unresolved gaps.
