# Plan a Fundraise

Build a decision-ready financing plan around the next value-inflecting milestone, not a headline amount or valuation.

## Load the source guidance

Read [references/source-map.md](references/source-map.md) before analyzing the raise. Use its cautions as boundaries, not as optional footnotes. Use [assets/fundraise-plan-template.md](assets/fundraise-plan-template.md) as the output skeleton unless the user requests another format.

## Gather inputs

Collect or derive:

- Company stage, business model, jurisdiction, and financing objective
- Current cash, restricted cash, monthly revenue, gross margin, burn, working-capital needs, and committed expenses
- Monthly operating model or enough detail to build base, delayed, and adverse cases
- One measurable financing-leverage milestone, its deadline, and the risks retired by reaching it
- Existing capitalization: issued equity, option pool, SAFEs, notes, warrants, debt, and material investor rights
- Proposed minimum, target, and maximum raise, if any
- Expected process duration, hiring plan, and operating buffer
- Plausible capital alternatives and whether another institutional round is part of the strategy

Label every input as reported, calculated, assumed, or unknown. Never invent missing numbers.

## Run the workflow

### 1. Define the outcome

Translate the founder's goal into a dated, observable milestone. Prefer a proof point that improves financing leverage: repeatable revenue, retention, margin, technical validation, regulatory progress, or credible proximity to cash-flow sustainability. Do not force break-even onto a business whose next rational proof point is different.

### 2. Build runway cases

Calculate months to the milestone in base, delayed, and adverse cases. Include fundraising lead time and a stated operating buffer. Show the monthly cash low point and the date at which management would need to change plan.

Do not equate a larger buffer with a better plan. Explain what incremental risk each additional dollar retires.

### 3. Size the round

Define:

- Minimum: independently funds a coherent plan; never use a cosmetic low target
- Target: funds the base plan plus a justified buffer
- Maximum: deploys additional capital into identified outcomes without undisciplined dilution or spend

Connect each amount to milestones, runway, hiring, and contingency actions. Separate uses of funds from the outcomes those uses are expected to create.

### 4. Model financing consequences

Compare at least the minimum and target cases. When inputs permit, include:

- Pre-money, new money, post-money, and new-investor ownership
- Option-pool treatment and fully diluted ownership
- SAFE and note conversion by instrument and form
- New cash relative to converting overhang
- Flat, up, and down next-round cases
- Founder and stakeholder dilution across more than one round

Keep SAFEs and notes legally and mechanically distinct. Treat the source's two-to-one conversion-overhang rule as a diagnostic flag, not a law. Do not calculate from category labels when the actual documents differ.

### 5. Compare broad structures

Compare priced equity, SAFE, convertible note, and relevant non-venture alternatives on present execution, ownership clarity, maturity or trigger risk, governance, next-round friction, and total cost. Recommend questions for counsel rather than drafting or interpreting legal terms.

### 6. Set timing and readiness gates

Work backward from the cash decision date. Identify the latest safe kickoff date, evidence that must exist before launch, owners, and weekly checkpoints. Include a contingency trigger before the company reaches a cash crisis.

### 7. Make the recommendation

State the preferred plan, why it dominates the alternatives, the assumptions most likely to reverse it, and the next three actions. Preserve a credible no-raise or delayed-raise option when the evidence supports one.

## Stop and ask

Stop and ask focused questions before giving a numeric recommendation when any of these are missing:

- Current unrestricted cash and a usable burn or cash-flow estimate
- A concrete milestone or decision point
- Existing convertibles, debt, or a current cap table needed for dilution analysis
- Jurisdiction and actual form/version when instrument mechanics matter

Stop calculations and request the governing documents when the user's summary conflicts with a cap table or term sheet. Do not fill gaps with market conventions.

Do not execute an offering, contact investors, select legal terms, or characterize a financing as compliant. Obtain explicit authorization before making external changes or sharing confidential information.

## Apply professional and freshness boundaries

Treat the output as educational planning, not legal, tax, accounting, securities, valuation, or investment advice. Direct the user to qualified professionals for actual documents, tax treatment, fiduciary duties, solicitation rules, and cap-table verification.

Do not reuse dated source figures for valuation, caps, discounts, note-versus-equity thresholds, process duration, anti-dilution prevalence, or market comparables. Verify current forms from official publishers and date any live market evidence. If current evidence is unavailable, say so and leave the assumption editable.

## Deliver the output

Produce one coherent plan with:

1. Decision summary and recommendation
2. Known inputs, assumptions, and missing evidence
3. Milestone and risk-retirement definition
4. Minimum/target/maximum raise table
5. Base/delayed/adverse runway cases
6. Financing and dilution scenarios, when supported
7. Timing, readiness gates, contingency triggers, and owners
8. Questions for legal, tax, accounting, or financial advisors
9. Immediate next actions
10. Handoff to pitch, process, equity, or modeling with the financing-plan
    artifact, missing documents, and provisional financing assumptions

Show formulas or calculation logic for all derived figures. Distinguish cash funded from soft interest or uncommitted capital.
