# Write an Investor Update

Create a durable operating communication that tells investors what changed, what management is doing, and where help can matter.

## Load the source guidance

Read [references/source-map.md](references/source-map.md) before drafting. Use [assets/investor-update-template.md](assets/investor-update-template.md) unless the user provides an established format. Do not reproduce source prose or quotations.

## Gather inputs

Collect:

- Audience, their information rights or sharing constraints, and preferred channel
- Reporting period, cadence, previous update, and current as-of date
- North-star and supporting metrics with definitions, plan, prior period, and actuals
- Cash, burn, runway, revenue, margin, retention, pipeline, and hiring data relevant to this company
- Wins, misses, material risks, decisions, and changes to forecast
- Progress against prior commitments and prior investor asks
- Specific introductions, expertise, recruiting, customer, financing, or decision support needed now
- Confidentiality, privilege, security, customer, personnel, regulatory, or material-information concerns

Treat reported numbers as unverified until they reconcile with the user's source of record. Never invent metrics, explanations, commitments, wins, or asks.

## Run the workflow

### 1. Establish continuity

Carry forward stable metric definitions, goals, prior commitments, and unresolved asks. Explain any definition or source change before comparing periods. Use the company's existing operating cadence rather than creating a disconnected reporting system.

### 2. Build the facts first

Create a compact metric table with current, prior, plan, variance, trend, and as-of date. Separate actuals from forecasts. For CAC, LTV, retention, or pipeline, show definitions and meaningful components instead of relying on a favorable blended figure.

If a number cannot be reconciled, label it provisional or omit it and state the follow-up owner/date.

### 3. Lead with the management summary

Summarize the period in a few direct sentences:

- What materially improved
- What materially missed or changed
- What management is doing next
- Where investor help is most useful

Do not bury bad news after highlights. Keep tone calm, factual, and accountable.

### 4. Report progress and misses

For each major operating area, state outcome, evidence, implication, owner, and next action. Close the loop on prior commitments. Avoid activity lists unless the activity explains an outcome or risk.

For adverse developments, distinguish facts, current interpretation, and unknowns. Explain impact, immediate mitigation, decision date, and next update. Surface the issue early enough for help to matter.

### 5. Make asks actionable

Write a small number of specific asks with target profile, context, desired action, constraints, and deadline. Never imply permission to forward confidential material. Make it easy for an investor to decline or ask for more context.

### 6. Preserve a durable record

Create a written version even when the founder prefers a narrated video. A video may add context, but pair it with the metric table, summary, asks, captions or transcript, and a secure archive. Save the final approved update for later diligence and board preparation according to the company's access policy.

### 7. Quality-check the update

Verify:

- Every metric has a stable definition and date
- Every forecast is labeled
- Wins and misses are balanced by materiality, not symmetry
- Risks have owners and next checkpoints
- Asks are specific and safe to forward only where intended
- Tone neither spins results nor sandbags targets
- The recipient list matches the information being shared

## Stop and ask

Stop and ask when the audience, period, as-of date, metric source, or approval status is unclear. Ask for the prior update when the user requests a recurring series but provides no baseline.

Stop before including:

- Privileged legal advice or active litigation detail
- Security incident information without an approved incident-communications path
- Customer, employee, health, financial, or other regulated personal information
- Material nonpublic information for a recipient who may not be entitled to it
- Unannounced financing terms, inaccurate runway, or unreconciled metrics

Ask who owns disclosure and whether counsel, finance, security, HR, or the board must review. Do not send, publish, forward, record, or upload the update without explicit authorization; drafting alone does not authorize delivery.

## Apply professional and freshness boundaries

Treat the update as communication support, not legal, tax, accounting, securities, privacy, or investment advice. Route disclosure duties, financial presentation, fundraising statements, investor rights, privilege, security incidents, and regulated information to qualified professionals.

Date financial and operating data. Refresh market benchmarks and fundraising claims before use. Do not carry forward stale source figures, definitions, investor lists, commitments, or asks merely because they appeared in a prior update.

## Deliver the output

Provide:

1. Subject line and reporting period
2. Short management summary
3. KPI table with plan/prior/current/variance and dates
4. Meaningful wins and learning
5. Misses, risks, decisions, owners, and checkpoints
6. Cash/runway and financing status when appropriate
7. Progress against prior commitments
8. Two or three specific asks
9. Priorities before the next update
10. Optional brief video outline only when useful
11. Handoff to the operating review or next financing cycle with decisions,
    open risks, commitments, and provisional data clearly labeled

Lead with the ready-to-review draft. After it, list provisional data, disclosure questions, and required approvers separately so they cannot be mistaken for update copy.
