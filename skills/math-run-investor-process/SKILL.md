# Run an Investor Process

Treat fundraising as a qualified relationship and sales process whose output is a survivable financing partner and complete deal, not merely the highest headline price.

## Load the source guidance

Read [references/source-map.md](references/source-map.md) before building the process. Use [assets/investor-process-template.md](assets/investor-process-template.md) as the working system unless the user supplies a CRM or preferred schema. Do not reproduce source prose or quotations.

## Gather inputs

Collect:

- Company stage, sector, model, geography, jurisdiction, round amount, instrument, and target close window
- Milestone the round funds and current runway decision date
- Ideal investor criteria: stage, check, ownership, thesis, geography, lead/follow behavior, reserves, and support needs
- Known conflicts, strategic-investor considerations, and unacceptable rights or behavior
- Existing network, advocates, prior investor conversations, and permission status for introductions
- Current target list and pipeline history
- Pitch, teaser, model, cap table, prior investor updates, and diligence materials
- Founder/team availability, meeting capacity, and decision owners
- Data sensitivity, access policy, and legal/security constraints

Distinguish researched facts, founder preferences, historical interactions, and assumptions. Never imply an introduction, endorsement, commitment, or fund fit that has not been verified.

## Run the workflow

### 1. Pass a readiness gate

Confirm that the narrative, core metrics, milestone plan, current cap table, and early diligence materials reconcile. List blocking gaps before outreach. Do not use strong introductions to compensate for missing fundamentals.

### 2. Define and verify investor fit

Create a scoring rubric that covers stage, check, thesis, portfolio adjacency, conflicts, partner relevance, decision process, reserves, reputation, board style, and strategic effects. Research the fund and individual partner separately.

Verify volatile facts from current first-party sources and record an as-of date. Treat investor databases as discovery aids, not final evidence. For a strategic investor, review information, governance, exclusivity, and exit-optionality risks with counsel rather than applying a simple count rule.

### 3. Build relationship paths

Map permissioned warm paths through investors, founders, operators, advisors, and existing shareholders. Ask potential advocates for advice first when the relationship is genuine; demonstrate progress over time. Never manufacture friendship, pressure a network, or draft language that implies an endorsement the connector did not give.

Maintain a targeted cold path for high-fit investors without access. Personalize with relevant investment evidence and a plain-language company case; avoid irrelevant personal details.

### 4. Prepare outreach

Create a short forwardable introduction, targeted cold note, one-page teaser, and clear meeting request from one consistent fact set. Adapt the deck policy to the investor's stated process. Test every statement about traction, pipeline, partnerships, and momentum.

Cluster outreach only when the company can handle the resulting meetings and diligence. Never present soft interest as committed or funded capital, and never manufacture urgency.

### 5. Operate the pipeline

Define stages with entry and exit criteria, for example:

1. Researched
2. Introduction requested or cold outreach ready
3. Contacted
4. First meeting scheduled/completed
5. Partner or follow-up meeting
6. Diligence
7. Terms under discussion
8. Closed, passed, paused, or declined by company

For every investor, track owner, last touch, next action/date, materials shared, objections, decision process, probability basis, conflict, and pass reason. Separate verbal interest, diligence, signed commitments, and funded cash.

### 6. Run meetings and follow-up

Prepare the investor's likely questions and the evidence appendix. Let questions change the deck sequence while answering directly. Send a prompt recap with agreed next steps, requested materials, owners, and dates. Use earlier advice and time-series progress honestly; never characterize an advice conversation as a commitment.

### 7. Stage diligence access

Maintain an early room and a deeper room. Assign each document an owner and update date. Apply least privilege, conflict checks, redaction, auditability, revocation, personal-data minimization, and secure handling of customer and trade-secret information. Mark non-applicable sections instead of silently omitting them.

Do not treat a casual email, legend, or presumed investor custom as adequate confidentiality protection. Obtain current legal and security guidance for sensitive disclosure.

### 8. Compare complete outcomes

Evaluate investor quality, governance, preferences, dilution, financing survivability, strategic constraints, and price together. Record reference checks and decision rationale. Do not recommend an investor solely because that investor moved fastest or offered the highest valuation.

## Stop and ask

Stop and ask when the round objective, runway decision date, investor profile, approved fact set, or sharing permissions are missing. Ask who can authorize outreach, introductions, data-room access, and term discussions.

Stop before any external action. Obtain explicit approval for the exact recipient and content before sending a message, requesting an introduction, scheduling a meeting, granting access, uploading materials, or changing a live CRM.

Stop if:

- A requested personalization would use irrelevant or sensitive personal data
- The target has a likely portfolio, commercial, or strategic conflict that has not been assessed
- Metrics or financing status conflict across materials
- Diligence would expose privileged, regulated, security-sensitive, customer, employee, or trade-secret information without an approved path
- The user asks for deceptive scarcity, fabricated commitments, or implied endorsements

## Apply professional and freshness boundaries

Treat this as process support, not legal, tax, accounting, securities, privacy, valuation, or investment advice. Route solicitation rules, offering communications, investor rights, board duties, strategic-investor conflicts, data sharing, confidentiality, and financing documents to qualified professionals.

Refresh fund status, partner role, thesis, portfolio, check size, geography, lead behavior, and contact route before live outreach. Date the evidence and mark anything not confirmed by a current first-party source. Treat process durations, meeting counts, and introduction volumes from source lessons as examples, not benchmarks or guarantees.

## Deliver the output

Provide:

1. Readiness decision and blocking gaps
2. Ideal-investor rubric and disqualifiers
3. Verified target and relationship-path table
4. Permissioned outreach kit
5. Pipeline stages, fields, owners, and operating cadence
6. First-meeting and follow-up checklist
7. Staged data-room index and access controls
8. Whole-deal decision rubric and risk register
9. The next seven days of actions, clearly separating drafts from approved external actions
10. Handoff to pitch iteration or investor updates with pipeline state,
    objections, approved claims, unresolved diligence, and next actions

Keep current state, next action, and evidence date visible. Never hide uncertainty inside a probability score.
