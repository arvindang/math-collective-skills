# MATH Validate Startup

Validate the problem before polishing the solution. Prefer customer behavior,
specific incidents, workarounds, and commitments over stated enthusiasm.

## Intake

Collect what is available:

- target user, buyer, beneficiary, and excluded segments;
- triggering situation, consequence, frequency, and current workaround;
- proposed change and why the company can deliver it unusually well;
- current evidence: interviews, usage, revenue, churn, pipeline, or experiments;
- decision deadline, experiment budget, and ethical or privacy constraints.

Ask only for missing facts that change the test. Label invented personas or
unverified market claims as hypotheses.

## Workflow

1. Write a plain-language problem statement a non-expert could repeat.
2. Build an evidence ledger: claim, observation, assumption, confidence, and
   next validation.
3. Classify urgency using consequence, workaround cost, existing spend,
   decision authority, and time sensitivity. Do not rely on the painkiller
   metaphor alone.
4. Design neutral interviews around the last real occurrence. Avoid asking
   whether users like the idea or would hypothetically buy it.
5. Define a commitment ladder: time, data, introduction, workflow change,
   pilot, deposit, contract, or repeat use, as appropriate and ethical.
6. Triangulate market size bottom-up from reachable customers, price/frequency,
   and acquisition capacity; compare with a dated top-down check where useful.
7. Define the smallest test, observation window, quality guardrails, and
   continue/change/stop thresholds before execution.
8. Record disconfirming evidence and alternate explanations explicitly.

Use [the validation plan](assets/startup-validation-plan.md).

## Output contract

- problem and customer definition;
- evidence ledger and contradiction list;
- interview or observation plan;
- commitment ladder;
- bottom-up market logic with assumptions and range;
- test design, owner, date, and thresholds;
- recommendation: validate further, build a narrow wedge, reposition, or stop.
- handoff: next skill, evidence ledger to carry forward, missing inputs, and
  assumptions that must not be promoted to facts.

Read [the source map](references/source-map.md) for the relevant lesson context.
End with **Watch/read next** and one to three direct links selected for the
weakest part of the user's evidence.

## Boundaries

- Do not treat a large market report or a percentage-of-market claim as proof.
- Do not manipulate users into commitments or expose sensitive customer data.
- Separate willingness to try from willingness and ability to pay.
- Do not generalize thin interviews or promotional archive pages into evidence.
- Treat pricing, revenue, and forecasts as tests with uncertainty, not promises.
